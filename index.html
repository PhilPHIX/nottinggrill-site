03The Story

restaurant level food,
back-yard energy.

Notting Grill started with a grill, a back yard in Notting Hill, and a simple idea: cook seasonal, local and wild food to a restaurant standard — but outdoors, over flame, with a YouTube channel for anyone who wants to follow our journey and learn with us.

No white tablecloths. Just smoke, good ingredients, and a genuine curiosity about where food comes from and the people who grow it.

The Founders

a filmmaker & a chef
walk into a bar…

Phil Sansom

Film Director, Photographer & Founder

An award winning film director and photographer who has worked with some of the world's biggest brands, from The Macallan to Bvlgari, adidas to Audi. Local to Notting Hill for over 30 years, he has helped create content for a host of celebrity chefs and hospitality brands. He runs content production company PHIX and is the founder of Notting Grill.

Philip Sansom & Justin Horne met at Electric House in Notting Hill. Longstanding local faces in the film and food industries, they hit it off and decided to combine the best of film and photography with the best of food and hospitality — making incredible restaurant-level food accessible to any garden cook.

A shared passion for fire cooking and a seasonal, ecologically activated approach to food became the foundation for Notting Grill. How we grow food and connect with nature is a vital story that needs telling through an accessible platform that can turn up the heat on regenerative practices.

Keeping it low-key and filming from the back yard in one of London's most iconic foodie neighbourhoods.

Collaborate with us

Justin Horne

Eco Chef, Author, Presenter & Forager

An eco chef, author, presenter, lecturer and avid forager. A longstanding west London food activist and member of Chefs' Manifesto and the UN SDG2 Advocacy Hub. In 2015 he launched Tiny Leaf, the first zero-waste, organic, plant-based restaurant in Europe, and was Chef Patron of Can Mimosa, a farm-to-table restaurant in Ibiza.

The Garden Studio

a back yard
in notting hill.

You've got to use what you have locally to make an impact — and we had a garden. Notting Grill is filmed from the back yard of one of London's most iconic foodie neighbourhoods, built around three simple spaces.

the grill

Where we cook over fire — using a wood-fired oven and a fire-made grill. Guest chefs and foodie friends cook alongside Justin, or take over entirely. We will deep dive into wood and charcoal selection, and the techniques that bring each dish to life.

the table

Where we share food, stories and conversation. This is where the best ideas happen and the best conversations begin — food turns to thought, thought turns to action. We'll host selected guests to eat what we produce and discuss a regenerative approach to food.

the bar

Exploring world-class libations of every kind — from local wines and beers to seasonal, wild-infused cocktails. Pairing suggestions and recommendations from producers we discover and ones we already love.

Why we focus on food

How we feed ourselves is the most direct impact we can all make, on the future of the planet.

Notting Grill was born from a passion for tackling some of the world's most pressing challenges — and few are bigger than farming, fishing and food production, which has the largest physical footprint of any industry on Earth. Half of all habitable land and ocean areas is now farmed and fished for human consumption.

How we transition from extraction, to a more circular economy, protect the stability of our climate, safeguard the more-than-human world  and unlock the health benefits of proper nutrition - is all bound up in how we farm and feed ourselves.

We've forgotten some of the basic principles of a balanced planetary ecosystem in favour of industrialised mass production. So our ambition is to explore how we might bring back some of the ancient wisdom of native and indigenous peoples, and merge it with the latest advances in technology and data capture.

Today food is politics. It's also at the cutting edge of science, biology, chemistry and ecology. How long we get to stay and play as a species corresponds directly to how we treat the most intelligent ecosystem on the planet — the three inches of microbiology living within our soil. The only place that turns death into life.

99% of life on Earth derives its energy from the sun, processed through photosynthesis by plants, enabled by the microbiology formed at the roots in the soil. No soil, no plants; no plants, no animals.

Wherever there are indigenous peoples, biodiversity is more resilient and abundant — we are ecosystem architects by nature. Manage these systems well and we can restore the damage we've done: build more resilient food systems, lower global temperatures, sequester more carbon, stabilise the climate, reactivate the water cycle, restore forests and wild landscapes, reverse the 73% decline in animal populations — and feed ourselves more nutritiously and deliciously in the process. Lets make nature great again. Re-wilding is the most import action we can take.
